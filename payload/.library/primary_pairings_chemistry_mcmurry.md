# Primary-Source Pairings: McMurry — Organic Chemistry

**Purpose:** After mastering a chapter in McMurry's *Organic Chemistry*, read the paired original source for historical depth. Use the warnings column to separate what still holds from what has been superseded.

| Textbook Chapter (McMurry) | Topic | Primary Source | Specific Section | Enrichment Value | ⚠️ What to Watch For (Outdated Concepts, Notation Shifts) | Free Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Structure and Bonding** | Chemical structure theory | Alexander Butlerov, "Einiges über die chemische Structur der Körper" (1861) | Zeitschrift für Chemie | The origin of the modern concept of chemical structure: atoms are joined in definite arrangements, and isomers differ by connectivity. | No electron-pair bonds (Lewis structures came later); no concept of hybridization or molecular orbitals; valence is empirical, not explained by quantum mechanics; aromaticity not yet understood. | [Internet Archive (German reprint)](https://archive.org/details/einigesberdieche00butl) |
| **2. Polar Covalent Bonds; Acids and Bases** | Lewis acid-base theory | Gilbert N. Lewis, *Valence and the Structure of Atoms and Molecules* (1923) | Chapter VI, "Acids and Bases" | The electron-pair definition of acids and bases — the foundation of most organic reactivity arguments. | Lewis' acid-base theory was slow to enter mainstream organic teaching; no reaction mechanisms with curved arrows (Ingold introduced those later); no pKa scale or solvent effects; proton transfer is treated qualitatively. | [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/valencestructure0000gilb) |
| **4. Organic Compounds: Cycloalkanes and Their Stereochemistry** | Ring strain | Adolf von Baeyer, "Über Polyacetylenverbindungen" (1885) | Berichte der deutschen chemischen Gesellschaft | The strain theory of ring formation: why small rings are unstable and why cyclopentane/cyclohexane are favored. | Baeyer assumed planar rings for all cycloalkanes; no knowledge of chair/boat conformations (Sachse–Mohr later); strain is qualitative, not quantified by force constants; thermodynamic data absent. | [Internet Archive (collected papers)](https://archive.org/details/baeyer collected works) |
| **5. Stereochemistry at Tetrahedral Centers** | Tetrahedral carbon | Jacobus Henricus van't Hoff, "A Suggestion Looking to the Extension into Space of the Structural Formulas at Present Used in Chemistry" (1874); Joseph Achille Le Bel, "Sur les relations..." (1874) | Bull. Soc. Chim. Fr. / van't Hoff pamphlet | The birth of stereochemistry: carbon as a tetrahedron, optical activity as a consequence of molecular asymmetry, and the prediction of enantiomers. | No absolute configuration (D/L and R/S systems came much later); van't Hoff's argument was based on optical activity, not X-ray crystallography; no Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules; Fischer projections not yet invented. | [Internet Archive (van't Hoff)](https://archive.org/details/chemistryinspace00vantrich) |
| **7. Alkenes: Structure and Reactivity** | Regioselectivity of addition | Vladimir Markovnikov, "On the Laws of Addition..." (1870) | Annalen der Chemie und Pharmacie | The empirical rule for addition of HX to alkenes: the halogen bonds to the more substituted carbon. | Markovnikov's rule is heuristic, not mechanistic; no carbocation intermediate (that explanation came with Ingold); exceptions (anti-Markovnikov, peroxide effect) were discovered later; no stereochemistry considered. | [Wikipedia summary with original references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markovnikov%27s_rule) |
| **11. Reactions of Alkyl Halides: Nucleophilic Substitutions and Eliminations** | Reaction mechanisms | Edward D. Hughes and Christopher Ingold, "Mechanism of Substitution at a Saturated Carbon Atom" (1937) | Journal of the Chemical Society | The SN1/SN2/E1/E2 mechanistic framework: kinetics, stereochemistry, and the role of solvent/nucleophile. | Early papers use kinetic order but no arrow-pushing notation in the modern sense; no computational transition-state theory; leaving-group ability and solvent effects are described empirically. | [Royal Society of Chemistry (paywalled; summaries widely available)](https://pubs.rsc.org/) |
| **13. Structure Determination: Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy** | NMR in condensed matter | Felix Bloch, William W. Hansen, Martin Packard, "The Nuclear Induction Experiment" (1946); Edward Mills Purcell, Henry C. Torrey, Robert V. Pound, "Resonance Absorption by Nuclear Magnetic Moments in a Solid" (1946) | *Physical Review* | The first detection of NMR in bulk matter — the physical basis of every modern ¹H/¹³C spectrum. | Early NMR used fixed-field/frequency sweeps, not Fourier-transform spectrometers; chemical shift was discovered shortly after (Proctor & Yu, Dickinson); no decoupling, COSY, or 2D methods; spectrometers were room-sized. | [Physical Review (AIP)](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.69.37) |
| **15. Benzene and Aromaticity** | Structure of benzene; aromaticity | August Kekulé, "Sur la constitution des substances aromatiques" (1865); Erich Hückel, "Quantentheoretische Beiträge zum Benzolproblem" (1931) | Bull. Soc. Chim. Fr. / *Zeitschrift für Physik* | Kekulé's oscillating ring and Hückel's 4n+2 π-electron rule — the empirical and quantum-mechanical pillars of aromaticity. | Kekulé's structure could not explain why all C–C bonds are equal; no resonance or molecular orbital theory in 1865; Hückel's method is a very simple LCAO-MO treatment; it fails for some nonbenzenoid aromatics and antiaromatics. | [Internet Archive (Kekulé biography/context)](https://archive.org/details/kekule00rich) |
| **17. Alcohols and Phenols** | Organomagnesium reagents | Victor Grignard, "Sur quelques nouvelles combinaisons organométaliques du magnésium..." (1900) | Comptes Rendus de l'Académie des Sciences | The discovery of Grignard reagents — one of the most general methods for forming carbon–carbon bonds and preparing alcohols. | Grignard's original substrates were limited; modern scope includes aryl, vinyl, and functionalized reagents; reaction conditions (anhydrous ether, controlled addition) were worked out empirically; no transition-metal catalysis. | [Internet Archive (French original via Gallica)](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k3101c) |
| **19. Aldehydes and Ketones: Nucleophilic Addition Reactions** | Wittig reaction | Georg Wittig, "Über Triphenyl-phosphin-methylene..." (1953) | *Chemische Berichte* | The Wittig reaction: converting carbonyls to alkenes with precise double-bond placement. | Original scope used simple ylides; stereoselectivity (Z vs. E) was not fully controlled until later (Schlosser, salt-free conditions); no modern catalytic variants; mechanism was debated for decades. | [Internet Archive (Wittig Nobel lecture)](https://archive.org/details/georgwittignobel00witt) |
| **23. Carbonyl Condensation Reactions** | Claisen condensation | Ludwig Claisen, "Über die Einwirkung von Natriumalkylat auf Verbindungen des Typus R.CO.CH2.CO.OR'" (1887) | *Chemische Berichte* | The classic condensation of esters to β-keto esters — a foundational carbon–carbon bond-forming reaction. | Conditions are harsh by modern standards (strong base, no temperature control); no discussion of enolate geometry or stereoselectivity; mixed condensations and crossed Claisen methods came later. | [Internet Archive (collected organic syntheses)](https://archive.org/details/organicsyntheses00colle) |
| **25. Biomolecules: Carbohydrates** | Sugar stereochemistry | Emil Fischer, "Ueber die Configuration des Traubenzuckers und seiner Isomeren" (1891) | *Berichte der deutschen chemischen Gesellschaft* | Fischer's proof of glucose stereochemistry and the invention of Fischer projections — the birth of modern carbohydrate chemistry. | Fischer had to assume the configuration of one sugar to fix the rest (later confirmed by X-ray); no conformational analysis (pyranose chair forms came with Haworth); cyclic hemiacetals were not yet fully understood. | [Internet Archive (Fischer papers)](https://archive.org/details/biologicalchemis00fisc) |
| **26. Biomolecules: Amino Acids, Peptides, and Proteins** | Protein sequencing | Frederick Sanger, "The Arrangement of Amino Acids in Proteins" (1952) / insulin sequence papers (1945–1955) | *Advances in Protein Chemistry* / *Biochemical Journal* | The first determination of a protein's primary structure (insulin), establishing that proteins have a defined sequence. | Sanger's 2,4-dinitrofluorobenzene method was laborious and required grams of protein; no mass spectrometry or Edman degradation; disulfide bridges were deduced by partial oxidation; no three-dimensional structure. | [Biochemical Journal (open access)](https://portlandpress.com/biochemj/article/351/3/617/56178) |
| **28. Biomolecules: Nucleic Acids** | DNA double helix | James Watson & Francis Crick, "A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid" (1953) | *Nature* 171, 737–738 | The one-page proposal of the double helix, base pairing, and the copying mechanism. | No mechanism for replication enzymes; no mention of messenger RNA; base-pairing rules are correct but the biological mechanism was speculative; Rosalind Franklin's data were used without full attribution. | [Nature (open access)](https://www.nature.com/articles/171737a0) |
| **30. Orbitals and Organic Chemistry: Pericyclic Reactions** | Conservation of orbital symmetry | Robert B. Woodward and Roald Hoffmann, "Stereochemistry of Electrocyclic Reactions" (1965) | *Journal of the American Chemical Society* | The Woodward–Hoffmann rules: predicting the stereochemistry of pericyclic reactions from orbital symmetry. | The rules were empirical generalizations supported by frontier-MO arguments; full rigor required later topological/quantum treatments; some reactions require state-correlation diagrams beyond simple HOMO-LUMO reasoning. | [ACS (paywalled; Nobel lecture freely available)](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1981/hoffmann/lecture/) |
| **31. Synthetic Polymers** | Macromolecular hypothesis | Hermann Staudinger, "Über Polymerisation" (1920) | *Berichte der deutschen chemischen Gesellschaft* | Staudinger's proposal that polymers are long covalent molecules (macromolecules), not aggregates of small molecules. | Early opposition from the colloidal school; molecular-weight methods were primitive; no mechanism of chain-growth vs. step-growth polymerization; stereoregularity (Ziegler–Natta catalysts) came decades later. | [Internet Archive (Staudinger collected works)](https://archive.org/details/fromorganicchemi00stau) |
