# Primary-Source Pairings: Campbell Biology

**Purpose:** After mastering a chapter in Campbell Biology, read the paired original source for historical depth. Use the warnings column to separate what still holds from what has been superseded.

| Textbook Chapter (Campbell) | Topic | Primary Source | Specific Section | Enrichment Value | ⚠️ What to Watch For (Outdated Concepts, Notation Shifts) | Free Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **22. Descent with Modification: A Darwinian View of Life** | Natural selection | Charles Darwin, *On the Origin of Species* (1859) | Chapters III–IV, "Struggle for Existence" and "Natural Selection" | The original argument for natural selection: variation, heredity, differential survival, and the tree of life. | No genetics — Darwin had no mechanism of inheritance (Mendel was unknown to him); blending inheritance was assumed by many contemporaries; the term "gene" does not exist; speciation mechanisms are largely absent. | [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/originofspecies00darw) |
| **14. Mendel and the Gene Idea** | Laws of inheritance | Gregor Mendel, "Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden" (1866) | Translated as "Experiments on Plant Hybrids" | The birth of genetics: dominance, segregation, independent assortment — derived from pea-plant counts. | No chromosomes; no concept of DNA; ratios given in integer counts without statistical tests; terms "gene" and "allele" are absent; Mendel's ratios were later found to be suspiciously clean. | [Internet Archive (English translation)](https://archive.org/details/experimentsinpla00mend) |
| **16. The Molecular Basis of Inheritance** | DNA as genetic material | Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, Maclyn McCarty, "Studies on the Chemical Nature of the Substance Inducing Transformation of Pneumococcal Types" (1944) | *Journal of Experimental Medicine* | The "transforming principle" experiment: DNA, not protein, carries genetic information. | No knowledge of DNA structure; the term "gene" is used but its physical nature is unclear; resistance to accepting DNA as the genetic material persisted until Hershey-Chase; no PCR or sequencing. | [PMC (open access)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2135440/) |
| **16. The Molecular Basis of Inheritance** | DNA as genetic material (confirmatory) | Alfred Hershey & Martha Chase, "Independent Functions of Viral Protein and Nucleic Acid in Growth of Bacteriophage" (1952) | *Journal of General Physiology* | The Waring blender experiment that convinced most biologists DNA is the hereditary molecule. | No electron-microscopy imagery of injection; radioisotope labeling was crude; the experiment did not rule out all protein roles, but it made DNA central. | [PMC (open access)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2142442/) |
| **16. The Molecular Basis of Inheritance** | DNA double helix | James Watson & Francis Crick, "A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid" (1953) | *Nature* 171, 737–738 | The one-page letter proposing the double helix, base pairing, and the copying mechanism. | No mechanism for replication enzymes; no mention of messenger RNA; base-pairing rules are correct but the biological mechanism was speculative; Rosalind Franklin's data were used without full attribution. | [Nature (open access)](https://www.nature.com/articles/171737a0) |
| **17. Gene Expression: From Gene to Protein** | Gene regulation | François Jacob & Jacques Monod, "Genetic Regulatory Mechanisms in the Synthesis of Proteins" (1961) | *Journal of Molecular Biology* | The lac operon model: how genes can be switched on and off by a repressor and inducer. | Proposed before the genetic code was cracked; assumes one gene → one enzyme; no concept of eukaryotic enhancers, splicing, or chromatin remodeling; mRNA was still being characterized. | [Internet Archive (reprint)](https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780226388327) |
| **17. Gene Expression: From Gene to Protein** | The genetic code | Marshall Nirenberg & Heinrich Matthaei, "The Dependence of Cell-Free Protein Synthesis in E. coli upon Naturally Occurring or Synthetic Polyribonucleotides" (1961) | *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* | The poly-U experiment: UUU codes for phenylalanine, the first codon cracked. | Cell-free systems were crude; only a few codons were assigned per experiment; the full code required years of work by many labs; no tRNA structures yet. | [PMC (open access)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC223181/) |
| **6. A Tour of the Cell** | Endosymbiotic origin of organelles | Lynn Margulis, "On the Origin of Mitosing Cells" (1967) | *Journal of Theoretical Biology* | The argument that mitochondria and chloroplasts were once free-living prokaryotes. | Initially rejected by mainstream biology; the paper mixes valid evidence with some speculative claims; modern phylogenetics has refined the story; serial endosymbiosis is now well accepted. | [ScienceDirect (paywall; summary widely available)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022519367900793) |
| **21. Genomes and Their Evolution** (or **Mobile Genetic Elements**) | Transposable elements | Barbara McClintock, "The Origin and Behavior of Mutable Loci in Maize" (1950) | *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* | Discovery of "controlling elements" — genes that move. | Terminology is maize-centric; no DNA sequencing to prove movement; the mechanism was mysterious; the significance was not widely accepted until decades later. | [PMC (open access)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1063007/) |
| **9. Cellular Respiration and Fermentation** | Citric acid cycle | Hans Krebs, "The Intermediate Metabolism of Carbohydrates" (1937) | *The Lancet* | The first coherent description of the citric acid (Krebs) cycle. | Isotope tracing was primitive; some intermediates were inferred rather than isolated; the full enzyme set was not yet known; regulation by ATP/ADP is absent. | [Internet Archive (Krebs biography/summary)](https://archive.org/details/remarkablemetabo00kreb) |
| **10. Photosynthesis** | Carbon fixation pathway | Melvin Calvin, "The Path of Carbon in Photosynthesis" (1950–1953, summarized in *Science* 1962) | *Science* 135, 879–889 | The Calvin cycle traced by ¹⁴C radiolabeling. | Early versions had the wrong order for some intermediates; the full enzyme set and regulation were worked out later; cyclic photophosphorylation was discovered separately. | [Internet Archive (review article)](https://archive.org/details/sim_science_1962-03-16_135_3507) |
| **7. Membrane Structure and Function** | Fluid mosaic model | S. Jonathan Singer & Garth Nicolson, "The Fluid Mosaic Model of the Structure of Cell Membranes" (1972) | *Science* 175, 720–731 | The first integrated model of the cell membrane as a lipid bilayer with embedded proteins. | The model has been refined: lipid rafts, membrane curvature, protein complexes, and cytoskeletal anchoring are later additions; the basic bilayer-plus-proteins picture holds. | [Internet Archive (review context)](https://archive.org/details/sim_science_1972-02-18_175_4023) |
