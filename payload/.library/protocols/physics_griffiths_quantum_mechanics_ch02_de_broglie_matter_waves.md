# Protocol: Louis de Broglie – *Recherches sur la théorie des quanta* (1924)

**Chapter:** 2 – Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation  
**Textbook:** Griffiths – *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Special relativity, wave–particle relations E = hν and p = h/λ, Hamilton–Jacobi theory (helpful but not required).

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** de Broglie’s doctoral thesis, defended at the Sorbonne on 25 November 1924 and published in *Annales de Physique* 10 (3), 22–128 (1925).
- **Free access:** [HAL / tel.archives-ouvertes (original French)](https://tel.archives-ouvertes.fr/tel-00006807/document)
- **Translation notes:** The English translation by A. F. Kracklauer (2004) is available but the French original is authoritative. Key terms: “onde de phase” = phase wave; “quantum” = energy element.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Schrödinger equation | De Broglie hypothesizes matter waves but does not write a wave equation for them. |
| Wavefunction interpretation | The phase wave is a relativistic phenomenon accompanying a particle, not a probability amplitude. |
| Quantum mechanics / operators | Energy and momentum are still classical–relativistic quantities; there are no operators or commutation relations. |
| Experimental confirmation of electron diffraction | Davisson–Germer and G. P. Thomson come in 1927; de Broglie’s hypothesis is theoretical in 1924. |
| Copenhagen interpretation | The thesis retains a realist, causal pilot-wave picture that de Broglie later abandons and then revives. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Starting from Einstein’s E = hν for photons and the relativistic energy–momentum relation E² = p²c² + m²c⁴, derive de Broglie’s relation λ = h/p for massive particles.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read Chapter 1 of the thesis, where de Broglie introduces the idea that every particle has an internal periodic phenomenon and an associated phase wave.
- [ ] **Close reading:** Follow the argument that the phase wave must travel faster than c (phase velocity v_phase = c²/v) while the particle energy corresponds to the wave frequency. Note how this avoids violating relativity.
- [ ] **Hydrogen atom:** Read de Broglie’s application to Bohr’s quantization condition: stable orbits require an integer number of wavelengths around the orbit (nλ = 2πr).
- [ ] **Derive:** Show that de Broglie’s standing-wave condition on a circular orbit reproduces Bohr’s angular-momentum quantization L = nℏ.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Understand that de Broglie was responding to the wave–particle duality of light and sought a symmetric treatment in which matter also has wave properties.
- [ ] **Reception task:** Einstein immediately recognized the importance of the idea; experimental confirmation by Davisson, Germer, and Thomson in 1927 turned the hypothesis into the foundation of wave mechanics.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The modern result is the de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p, which underlies the Schrödinger equation and all of wave mechanics.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern λ = h/p is a kinematic relation between momentum and wavelength for a quantum particle. De Broglie’s original reasoning is relativistic and mechanical: a massive particle has an internal clock and a phase wave that guides it. The modern relation strips away the pilot-wave and relativistic machinery.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- L. de Broglie, Nobel Lecture “The Wave Nature of the Electron” (1929) – his own later retrospective.
- G. Bacciagaluppi & A. Valentini, *Quantum Theory at the Crossroads* – historical analysis of the 1927 Solvay discussions of de Broglie’s pilot wave.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Thinking de Broglie derived the Schrödinger equation; he did not.
- Confusing phase velocity with group velocity; the particle velocity corresponds to the group velocity of the wave packet.
- Reading the modern probability interpretation back into the thesis; de Broglie’s ψ-like object is a physical phase wave.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Schrödinger 1926 Part I protocol (the wave equation that realizes de Broglie’s idea).
- Davisson–Germer 1927 / G. P. Thomson 1927 protocols (recommended: experimental confirmation).
- Bohr 1913 protocol (prerequisite atomic model).

## 8. Epistemic Safety Checklist (Post-Reading)

- [ ] I can state, in de Broglie’s own terms, why a material particle should have an associated wave.
- [ ] I can identify at least one tool or concept de Broglie lacked that I used to write the Schrödinger equation.
- [ ] I can describe Einstein’s role in publicizing the idea and the 1927 experiments that confirmed it.
- [ ] I can name what the modern λ = h/p relation obscures about de Broglie’s phase-wave reasoning.
- [ ] I have not used the phrase “basically the same as” or “just a primitive version of” in my notes.
