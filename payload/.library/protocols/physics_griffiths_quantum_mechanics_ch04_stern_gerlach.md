# Protocol: Walther Gerlach & Otto Stern – *Der experimentelle Nachweis der Richtungsquantelung im Magnetfeld* (1922)

**Chapter:** 4 – Quantum Mechanics in Three Dimensions  
**Textbook:** Griffiths – *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*  
**Tier:** Recommended  
**Prerequisites:** Angular momentum operators, eigenvalues of L² and L_z, magnetic moment of a current loop, basic atomic structure.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Gerlach & Stern, “Der experimentelle Nachweis der Richtungsquantelung im Magnetfeld,” *Zeitschrift für Physik* 9, 349–352 (1922).
- **Free access:** [Springer (DOI:10.1007/BF01326984)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01326984)
- **Local copy:** `.library/source_papers/gerlach_stern_1922.pdf`
- **Translation notes:** The title translates as “The Experimental Proof of Directional Quantization in the Magnetic Field.” The paper reports a two-line splitting of a silver atomic beam, which the authors interpret as space quantization.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Electron spin | The magnetic moment is attributed to orbital motion; the two-line splitting is not explained as spin-up/spin-down. |
| Quantum number s = ½ | No concept of a two-valued internal degree of freedom; spinors are absent. |
| Schrödinger equation / wave mechanics | The experiment is interpreted within the old quantum theory of Bohr and Sommerfeld. |
| Quantum measurement theory | The splitting is a classical-looking spatial separation, not yet a measurement of a discrete observable. |
| Atomic beam technology | The beam is produced by an oven and collimating slits; modern coherent, spin-polarized beams did not exist. |
| Modern Stern–Gerlach analysis | There is no discussion of eigenstates, superposition, or sequential SG measurements. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Write the classical expectation for a magnetic dipole in an inhomogeneous magnetic field: F_z = μ_z (∂B_z/∂z). Classically, μ_z should take a continuous range of values.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read the experimental description: silver atoms evaporated from an oven, collimated into a beam, passed through an inhomogeneous magnetic field, and detected on a glass plate.
- [ ] **Close reading:** Identify the reported result: two distinct lines rather than a continuous smear. Note the authors’ conclusion that this proves “space quantization.”
- [ ] **Quantization check:** For orbital angular momentum with quantum number ℓ, the number of m_ℓ values is 2ℓ + 1. Ask yourself: does a two-line pattern fit a pure orbital model? Why or why not?
- [ ] **Modern reinterpretation:** A silver atom has one valence electron in an s-state (ℓ = 0). Explain why the magnetic moment is due to electron spin and why only two orientations appear.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Explain why the result was puzzling in 1922: the old quantum theory expected space quantization of orbital angular momentum, but the observed two-line pattern did not match a simple orbital model.
- [ ] **Reception task:** Note that the spin hypothesis (Uhlenbeck & Goudsmit, 1925–26) retrospectively explained the two-line splitting as evidence of spin-½.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The modern analog is the Stern–Gerlach experiment as a measurement of the spin component S_z. For a spin-½ particle, the eigenvalues are ±ℏ/2, and the beam splits into two components corresponding to the eigenstates |↑⟩ and |↓⟩.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern interpretation uses the quantum theory of angular momentum, spinors, and the measurement postulate. Gerlach and Stern had none of these; they saw two lines and interpreted them within the old quantum theory as space quantization. The conceptual jump from “two lines” to “eigenvalues of a spin operator” took several years.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- J. J. Sakurai, *Modern Quantum Mechanics*, §1.1 – a clean modern treatment of the Stern–Gerlach experiment as a spin measurement.
- F. Weinert, “Wrong theory—right experiment: The significance of the Stern–Gerlach experiments” (*Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics*, 1995).

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Describing the experiment as if Gerlach and Stern already knew about electron spin; spin was proposed three years later.
- Assuming the two lines directly prove spin-½; the original authors interpreted them as space quantization of orbital angular momentum.
- Ignoring the fact that silver atoms are used precisely because their orbital angular momentum is zero, so the effect is purely spin.
- Confusing the 1922 silver experiment with later, more refined spin-polarization experiments.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Pauli 1925 protocol (the exclusion principle, which uses the same fourth quantum number later identified as spin).
- Dirac 1928 protocol (where spin emerges automatically from the relativistic equation).
- Bell 1964 protocol (where spin-½ measurements are used to test local hidden-variable theories).

## 8. Epistemic Safety Checklist (Post-Reading)

- [ ] I can describe the experimental setup and the result in Gerlach and Stern’s own terms.
- [ ] I can identify at least one tool or concept they lacked that I used to explain the two-line pattern.
- [ ] I can explain why a pure orbital-angular-momentum model does not naturally give two lines for silver.
- [ ] I can name what the modern spin-½ measurement interpretation obscures about the 1922 experiment.
- [ ] I have not used the phrase “basically the same as” or “just a primitive version of” in my notes.
