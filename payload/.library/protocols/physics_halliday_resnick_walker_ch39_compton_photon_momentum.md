# Protocol: Arthur H. Compton – *A Quantum Theory of the Scattering of X-rays by Light Elements* (1923)

**Chapter:** 39 – Photons and Matter Waves  
**Textbook:** Halliday, Resnick, Walker – *Fundamentals of Physics*  
**Tier:** Recommended  
**Prerequisites:** Photon energy E = hν, relativistic energy and momentum, conservation laws in two-body collisions, X-ray diffraction/Bragg scattering.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Compton’s paper in *Physical Review* 21 (5), 483–502 (May 1923).
- **Free access:** [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRev.21.483](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.21.483) (published 1923; public domain in the United States; copies widely available through libraries and repositories)
- **Translation notes:** Written in English. Compton uses “quantum” for what we now call a photon; “modified” and “unmodified” lines refer to the Compton-shifted and unshifted scattered radiation.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Quantum electrodynamics (QED) | Compton treats the collision as a billiard-ball encounter between a light quantum and an electron; there are no field operators or Feynman diagrams. |
| Klein–Nishina formula | The differential cross section is not computed; only the kinematic wavelength shift is derived and compared with experiment. |
| Dirac equation / spin | The recoil electron is treated as a free classical or semi-classical particle; spin and relativistic wave mechanics are absent. |
| Wave-particle duality formalism | Compton argues for the particle picture against the wave picture; the synthesis (wavefunction + field quantisation) does not yet exist. |
| Photomultipliers or modern detectors | Intensities are measured with an ionisation chamber and a Bragg spectrometer; statistical precision is limited. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Derive the wavelength shift for a photon scattering off a free electron at rest using relativistic energy and momentum conservation. Verify Δλ = (h/mₑc)(1 − cos φ).
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read the abstract and introduction, where Compton states his central hypothesis: an X-ray quantum transfers all its energy and momentum to a *single* electron, which then reradiates.
- [ ] **Theoretical section:** Follow Compton’s derivation of the scattering angle–wavelength relation. Note his use of the Doppler effect for a moving electron and the conservation equations.
- [ ] **Experimental section:** Examine the spectra of molybdenum Kα radiation scattered from graphite at 90°. Identify the “modified” (shifted) and “unmodified” (unshifted) peaks.
- [ ] **Derive:** Show that Compton’s formula predicts the observed shift at 90° and that the unmodified line arises from scattering by tightly bound inner electrons (effectively the whole atom).
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Identify the opposition: the classical wave theory predicted no wavelength shift for scattered X-rays; Compton’s particle model was contested by Duane, Ross, and others before Wilson’s cloud-chamber tracks clinched the case.
- [ ] **Reception task:** The Compton effect became the decisive empirical argument for the photon’s momentum; it figured centrally in the 1927 Solvay debates and helped motivate quantum mechanics.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

Modern Compton scattering is a tree-level QED process; the lowest-order amplitude is the Klein–Nishina formula.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern treatment describes the scattering as an interaction between quantised electron and photon fields, with creation and annihilation operators. Compton’s paper gives a *kinematic* particle model, not a *dynamical* field theory; the billiard-ball imagery obscures the probabilistic, field-based nature of the process.

## 5. Secondary Sources

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Thinking Compton discovered the photon; Einstein introduced light quanta in 1905, and Compton provided direct evidence for their momentum.
- Forgetting that the unmodified line is also present in Compton’s data; it is explained by scattering from bound atomic electrons, not by a failure of the quantum model.
- Applying the Klein–Nishina formula to 1923 data; Compton only derives and tests the wavelength shift, not the cross section.
