# Protocol: Paul A. M. Dirac – *The Quantum Theory of the Emission and Absorption of Radiation* (1927)

**Chapter:** 9 – Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory  
**Textbook:** Griffiths – *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Time-dependent perturbation theory, Fermi’s golden rule, harmonic oscillator quantization, Einstein A and B coefficients, electromagnetic plane waves.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Dirac’s paper, *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 114, 243–265 (1927).
- **Free access:** [Royal Society (DOI:10.1098/rspa.1927.0039)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1927.0039)
- **Translation notes:** Written in English. Dirac introduces creation (b_r^*) and annihilation (b_r) operators for photons; his notation differs from modern conventions but the structure is recognizable.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Relativistic quantum mechanics for electrons | Dirac’s equation for the electron comes in 1928; here the atom is treated non-relativistically. |
| Renormalization | Self-energy divergences are noted but not systematically removed; QED as a calculational framework matures only in the late 1940s. |
| Feynman diagrams | Transitions are calculated with matrix elements and perturbation theory, not diagrams. |
| Modern laser theory | The electromagnetic field is a collection of harmonic oscillators; coherent states are not yet introduced. |
| Antiparticles / positron theory | The negative-energy sea and pair production are absent; photons are the only quantized particles besides atomic electrons. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Derive Fermi’s golden rule for a sinusoidal perturbation and write Einstein’s A and B coefficients for a two-level atom interacting with radiation.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read the introduction, where Dirac states his goal: to quantize the electromagnetic field and derive Einstein’s coefficients from quantum mechanics.
- [ ] **Close reading:** Follow Dirac’s quantization of the radiation field into harmonic oscillators, one per mode. Identify the creation and annihilation operators and the commutation relation [b_r, b_r^*] = 1.
- [ ] **Emission and absorption:** Trace the matrix elements for absorption (atom goes up, one photon destroyed) and stimulated emission (atom goes down, one photon created).
- [ ] **Derive:** Show that the Einstein B coefficients for absorption and stimulated emission are equal, and relate the spontaneous emission rate to the density of photon states.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Understand that Dirac is unifying two viewpoints: the wave description of light (Maxwell) and the light-quantum description (Einstein). He shows they give the same Hamiltonian.
- [ ] **Reception task:** This paper is widely regarded as the birth of quantum electrodynamics. It was extended by Jordan, Wigner, Heisenberg, Pauli, and finally given its modern form by Feynman, Schwinger, and Tomonaga.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The modern result is QED perturbation theory for atom–photon interactions: the interaction Hamiltonian H' ∝ p·A + A², and transition rates are computed with Fermi’s golden rule using photon creation/annihilation operators.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern treatment is relativistic, covariant, and uses Feynman diagrams and renormalization. Dirac’s paper is non-relativistic, Hamiltonian, and perturbative in the old sense; the physical picture of photons as quantized oscillator excitations is central.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- S. Weinberg, *The Quantum Theory of Fields*, Vol. 1 – modern treatment of the canonical quantization of the electromagnetic field.
- A. Pais, *Inward Bound* – historical account of the emergence of QED.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Confusing Dirac’s 1927 radiation theory with his 1928 electron equation; they are different papers.
- Expecting the full apparatus of modern QED; this is the first step, not the mature theory.
- Overlooking that Dirac quantizes the field but treats the atom as a non-relativistic two-level system.
