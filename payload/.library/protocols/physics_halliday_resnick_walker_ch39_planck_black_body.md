# Protocol: Max Planck – *On the Law of Distribution of Energy in the Normal Spectrum* (1901)

**Chapter:** 39 – Photons and Matter Waves  
**Textbook:** Halliday, Resnick, Walker – *Fundamentals of Physics*  
**Tier:** Recommended  
**Prerequisites:** Black-body radiation, Wien’s displacement law, Stefan–Boltzmann law, elementary statistical mechanics, electromagnetic oscillators.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Planck’s 1901 paper in *Annalen der Physik* 4, 553–563, “Über das Gesetz der Energieverteilung im Normalspectrum.” The most accessible faithful English translation is Patrick Marc’s 2024 version on Zenodo.
- **Free access:** [Zenodo – English translation (DOI:10.5281/zenodo.10520385)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10520385)
- **Translation notes:** Planck’s “Resonator” is a harmonic oscillator coupled to the radiation field; “Energiequant” = energy quantum. The paper’s goal is to *derive* the black-body spectrum, not to propose a general quantum theory of light.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Photon concept | Planck quantizes the *material oscillators* in the cavity walls, not the electromagnetic field itself. |
| Quantum mechanics or Schrödinger equation | The resonator is treated classically except for the ad hoc restriction that its energy is an integer multiple of hν. |
| Bose–Einstein statistics | Planck’s counting of oscillator states is a semiclassical Boltzmann-style argument; the correct bosonic counting comes in 1924. |
| Zero-point energy | The lowest allowed oscillator energy is E = 0 in 1901; the ½hν ground state appears only later. |
| Relativity | The paper is non-relativistic and treats the ether as a classical continuum. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Derive the Rayleigh–Jeans law from the equipartition theorem and note where it diverges at short wavelengths (the ultraviolet catastrophe).
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read Planck’s opening, where he summarises his previous attempts to improve Wien’s law and states the empirical formula that fits the data.
- [ ] **Entropy argument:** Follow Planck’s derivation of the entropy of a resonator from the spectral energy density. Note how he constructs the entropy from combinatorial considerations.
- [ ] **Quantisation step:** Locate the point where Planck introduces the hypothesis that the resonator energy can only take discrete values E = n hν. Observe that this is presented as a mathematical device, not a physical claim about light.
- [ ] **Derive:** Reconstruct Planck’s route from the entropy expression to the black-body spectral energy density ρ(ν,T) = (8πhν³/c³) / (e^(hν/kT) − 1).
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Understand that Planck was trying to save thermodynamic consistency for cavity radiation, not to overthrow classical physics; he spent years attempting to derive h from classical principles.
- [ ] **Reception task:** The formula was quickly accepted because it fit experiment, but the energy-quantum hypothesis was initially treated by most physicists as a calculational trick.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The modern result is Planck’s black-body law, now derived via Bose–Einstein statistics for photons in a cavity.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern derivation quantizes the electromagnetic field directly and treats photons as bosons. Planck’s 1901 paper quantizes material oscillators and retains classical electromagnetism; the physical meaning of h as a universal constant of action is only glimpsed, not asserted.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- T. S. Kuhn, *Black-Body Theory and the Quantum Discontinuity, 1894–1912* – the standard historical analysis.
- M. Planck, *The Theory of Heat Radiation* (1914; Dover reprint) – Planck’s own later synthesis.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Attributing the photon concept to Planck; Einstein introduced light quanta in 1905.
- Thinking Planck quantised light itself in 1901; he quantised the energy of cavity oscillators.
- Reading the modern black-body derivation back into the paper and dismissing Planck’s entropy argument as unnecessary.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Einstein 1905 photoelectric-effect protocol (light quanta).
- Compton 1923 protocol (photon momentum).
- Bose 1924 / Einstein 1924–25 protocols (advanced: correct quantum statistics).
