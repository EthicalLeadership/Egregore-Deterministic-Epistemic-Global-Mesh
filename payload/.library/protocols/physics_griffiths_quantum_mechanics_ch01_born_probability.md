# Protocol: Max Born – *Zur Quantenmechanik der Stoßvorgänge* (1926)

**Chapter:** 1 – The Wave Function  
**Textbook:** Griffiths – *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Complex numbers, wavefunction normalization, Schrödinger equation, basic probability theory.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Born’s short communication, *Zeitschrift für Physik* 37, 863–867 (1926). The probabilistic interpretation appears as a footnote-added-in-proof and is expanded in the longer follow-up, *Zeitschrift für Physik* 38, 803–827 (1926).
- **Free access:** [Springer (DOI:10.1007/BF01397477)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01397477)
- **Translation notes:** The German “Häufigkeit” (frequency of occurrence) is translated as probability; Born uses Schrödinger’s ψ for material particles, not light.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| General postulate of the Born rule | The interpretation emerges as a footnote in a scattering paper, not as an axiom of measurement theory. |
| Hilbert-space formalism | ψ is a wavefunction in coordinate space; there is no abstract state vector or Dirac notation. |
| Measurement theory / collapse postulate | Born speaks of the probability of a given effect of a collision, not of wavefunction collapse. |
| Ensemble vs. single-system interpretation | Born is ambivalent; later writers (von Neumann, Einstein) sharpen the distinction. |
| Modern scattering theory (S-matrix, cross sections) | The collision problem is treated perturbatively; no general scattering formalism yet exists. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Write down the normalization condition ∫|ψ(x)|² dx = 1 and interpret it as a probability density. Note that this interpretation is not forced by the Schrödinger equation itself.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read Born’s opening, where he contrasts the old question “What is the state after the collision?” with the new quantum-mechanical question “How probable is a given effect?”
- [ ] **Close reading:** Locate the footnote (or passage) in which |ψ|² is identified with the probability density for finding a particle. Note that it appears almost incidentally.
- [ ] **Scattering context:** Understand why Born introduces the probabilistic interpretation in the context of a collision: the final state is not determined, only the probabilities of various outgoing states.
- [ ] **Derive:** For a plane wave e^{ikx}, verify that |ψ|² is constant and interpret this as a uniform probability density. For a wave packet, relate the width in position space to the width in momentum space.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Identify the intellectual pressure: Schrödinger had hoped ψ represented a continuous charge distribution; Born’s probabilistic reading defeats that interpretation.
- [ ] **Reception task:** Einstein’s December 1926 letter to Born (“God does not play dice”) shows that the probabilistic interpretation was immediately controversial, not universally accepted.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The modern postulate: for a normalized wavefunction ψ(x,t), the probability density is ρ(x,t) = |ψ(x,t)|² and the probability of finding the particle in [a,b] is ∫_a^b |ψ|² dx.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern Born rule is a general postulate applicable to any observable and any measurement. Born’s original statement is tied to scattering and coordinate-space wavefunctions; the step from “probability density in space” to “probability of eigenvalues for arbitrary observables” is missing.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- J. Mehra & H. Rechenberg, *The Historical Development of Quantum Theory*, Vol. 6 – detailed context of Born’s 1926 papers.
- M. Beller, “Born’s Probabilistic Interpretation: A Case Study of Concepts in Flux” (*Studies in History and Philosophy of Science*, 1990).

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Attributing the full Born rule (for arbitrary observables) to this paper; that generalization came later, especially through von Neumann.
- Thinking Born derived the rule from deeper principles; he proposed it as a consistent interpretation of scattering experiments.
- Confusing Born’s probability density with Schrödinger’s earlier charge-density interpretation.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Schrödinger 1926 Part I protocol (the wavefunction whose meaning Born interprets).
- Heisenberg 1925 protocol (the alternative matrix formulation).
- von Neumann 1932 / Dirac 1930 protocols (advanced: measurement theory and axiomatic QM).

## 8. Epistemic Safety Checklist (Post-Reading)

- [ ] I can state, in Born’s own terms, what question quantum mechanics answers for a collision process.
- [ ] I can identify at least one tool or concept Born lacked that I used to state the general Born rule.
- [ ] I can describe Schrödinger’s alternative interpretation and why Born rejected it.
- [ ] I can name what the modern measurement postulate obscures about Born’s original scattering-based reasoning.
- [ ] I have not used the phrase “basically the same as” or “just a primitive version of” in my notes.
