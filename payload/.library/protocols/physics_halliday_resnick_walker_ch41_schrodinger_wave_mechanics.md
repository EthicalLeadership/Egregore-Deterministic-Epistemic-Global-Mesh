# Protocol: Erwin Schrödinger – *Quantisation as a Problem of Proper Values* (1926)

**Chapter:** 41 – All About Atoms  
**Textbook:** Halliday, Resnick, Walker – *Fundamentals of Physics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Bohr’s model (Protocol 13), de Broglie hypothesis, wave-particle duality, differential equations.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** English collection *Collected Papers on Wave Mechanics* (1928), translated by J. F. Shearer. Also widely reproduced in source collections.
- **Free access:** [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/collectedpaperso00schr)
- **Translation notes:** The English translation is standard but read with awareness that key terms (*Eigenwert* → proper value, *Eigenfunktion* → proper function) carry mathematical connotations.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Born’s probability interpretation (1926) | Schrödinger believed \(\psi\) represented a real physical matter wave, not a probability amplitude; \(|\psi|^2\) as probability density came from Born months later |
| Heisenberg uncertainty principle (1927) | The wavefunction is treated as a complete description without complementarity constraints |
| Relativistic quantum mechanics (Dirac, 1928) | The equation is non-relativistic; spin is not included |
| Operator formalism / Hilbert space | He uses differential equations and boundary conditions, not abstract operator theory |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Write down the time-independent Schrödinger equation for hydrogen from memory. Then set it aside — Schrödinger arrived at it through a different path.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read Part I of the 1926 paper (the hydrogen atom solution). Focus on how Schrödinger treats quantization as an eigenvalue problem of a wave equation, not as an ad hoc postulate.
- [ ] **Standing-wave analogy:** Track his analogy to standing waves on a string: the hydrogen energy levels emerge as the allowed frequencies of a standing matter wave in a Coulomb potential.
- [ ] **Derive levels:** Note that he derives the Bohr energy levels (\(E_n = -13.6 \text{ eV}/n^2\)) from the wave equation, but with proper quantum numbers that emerge naturally from boundary conditions, not from ad hoc assumptions.
- [ ] **Rejection of jumps:** Observe his explicit rejection of the quantum jump: he argues that transitions are continuous wave processes, not discrete jumps between orbits.
- [ ] **Critical note:** Schrödinger initially interpreted \(|\psi|^2\) as charge density, not probability density. The modern probabilistic interpretation is Born’s (1926) and was resisted by Schrödinger.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Identify what Schrödinger is arguing against — the ad hoc postulates of Bohr’s model and the abstract matrix mechanics of Heisenberg (1925).
- [ ] **Reception task:** The mathematical power of wave mechanics was immediately recognized, but Schrödinger’s physical interpretation (real matter waves) was rejected in favor of Born’s probabilistic interpretation after intense debate at the 1927 Solvay Conference.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The time-independent Schrödinger equation: \(\hat{H}\psi = E\psi\). For hydrogen: \(E_n = -13.6 \text{ eV}/n^2\). The model generalizes to multi-electron atoms (approximately) and molecules.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern Copenhagen interpretation treats \(\psi\) as a probability amplitude and the equation as a postulate of quantum mechanics. Schrödinger derived the equation from a classical wave analogy and believed \(\psi\) described a real, continuous matter wave. The modern form obscures his wave-monist, realist ontology and the fact that he saw his theory as a return to classical continuity, not a departure from it.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- David Griffiths, *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics* – modern pedagogical treatment.
- Abraham Pais, *Niels Bohr’s Times* – context on the Bohr-Schrödinger-Heisenberg debates.
- Erwin Schrödinger, *Collected Papers on Wave Mechanics* — his own words on interpretation.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Retrojecting Born’s probabilistic interpretation onto Schrödinger’s 1926 paper; he explicitly rejected it.
- Treating the Schrödinger equation as an axiom; it was derived from a classical wave analogy and Hamilton-Jacobi mechanics.
- Assuming Schrödinger accepted quantum jumps; he argued they were unnecessary and unphysical.
- Applying the modern operator formalism; Schrödinger uses differential equations and boundary conditions.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Heisenberg’s matrix mechanics (1925) — the alternative formulation.
- Born’s probabilistic interpretation (1926) — the reinterpretation Schrödinger resisted.
- Dirac’s relativistic equation (1928) — the unification of quantum mechanics and relativity.

## 8. Epistemic Safety Checklist (Post-Reading)

- [ ] I can state Schrödinger’s argument in his own terms: eigenvalue problem, standing matter waves, rejection of quantum jumps.
- [ ] I can identify at least one tool Schrödinger lacked that I used to understand his result.
- [ ] I can describe the 1926–1927 debate over interpretation and Schrödinger’s resistance to the probabilistic view.
- [ ] I can name what the modern Copenhagen/operator formalism obscures about Schrödinger’s wave-realist derivation.
- [ ] I have not used the phrase “basically the same as” or “just a primitive version of” in my notes.
