# Protocol: Paul A. M. Dirac – *The Quantum Theory of the Electron* (1928)

**Chapter:** Special Topic – Relativistic Quantum Mechanics  
**Textbook:** Griffiths – *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*  
**Tier:** Recommended  
**Prerequisites:** Special relativity (E² = p²c² + m²c⁴), Pauli matrices and spin-½, the Schrödinger equation, familiarity with complex matrices.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Dirac, P. A. M., “The Quantum Theory of the Electron,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 117, 610–624 (1928); and Part II, *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 118, 351–361 (1928).
- **Free access:** [Royal Society Part I (DOI:10.1098/rspa.1928.0023)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1928.0023); [Royal Society Part II (DOI:10.1098/rspa.1928.0056)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1928.0056)
- **Local copies:** `.library/source_papers/dirac_1928_part1.pdf`, `.library/source_papers/dirac_1928_part2.pdf`
- **Translation notes:** The papers are in English. Part I derives the equation and shows that spin and the magnetic moment emerge automatically; Part II interprets the negative-energy states and develops the hole theory.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Positron as observed particle | Anderson discovered the positron in 1932; Dirac initially hesitated to identify the “hole” with a positive electron. |
| Quantum electrodynamics | The Dirac equation is for a single electron in a fixed field; photon emission/absorption and renormalization come later. |
| Renormalization | Divergences in self-energy and vacuum polarization are not addressed. |
| Fock-space / second quantization | The equation is first-quantized; the modern interpretation uses field operators and particle creation/annihilation. |
| Standard Model | No weak or strong interactions; no quarks or gauge bosons beyond the photon. |
| Hole theory as finished product | Hole theory is developed in Part II but is conceptually awkward; modern QED replaces it with Fock-space antimatter. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Write the relativistic energy-momentum relation E² = p²c² + m²c⁴ and explain why a naive square-root Hamiltonian H = √(p²c² + m²c⁴) is problematic for quantum mechanics.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read Dirac’s introduction, where he argues that the Klein–Gordon equation is unsatisfactory because it is second-order in time and gives a non-positive probability density.
- [ ] **Close reading:** Follow Dirac’s factorization E² − p²c² − m²c⁴ = (E − α·pc − βmc²)(E + α·pc + βmc²) and the conditions on the matrices α_i and β.
- [ ] **Derive:** Show that the α_i and β must be at least 4×4 matrices and that the Dirac equation is first-order in both space and time.
- [ ] **Spin and magnetic moment:** Verify that the orbital angular momentum L is not conserved, but the total angular momentum J = L + S is conserved, where S = (ℏ/2)Σ and Σ_i are block-diagonal Pauli matrices. Show that the g-factor is 2.
- [ ] **Negative energies:** Identify the negative-energy solutions. Explain why they are not simply discarded and how Dirac interprets them in Part II as holes in a filled sea.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Explain why the automatic emergence of spin was considered a triumph: spin had previously been added to the Schrödinger equation by hand via the Pauli Hamiltonian.
- [ ] **Reception task:** Note that the positron’s discovery validated the physical content of the negative-energy states, even though the hole-theory interpretation was later superseded by QED.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The modern analog is the Dirac field in quantum electrodynamics: a relativistic spin-½ field whose excitations are electrons and positrons. In a fixed classical electromagnetic field, the first-quantized Dirac equation still describes the electron accurately except at very high energies or strong fields.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern view treats particles and antiparticles as excitations of a quantum field. Dirac’s 1928 paper is first-quantized and uses the hole-theory interpretation; the negative-energy sea is a classical-looking device that modern QED replaces with Fock-space creation and annihilation operators.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- S. Weinberg, *The Quantum Theory of Fields*, Vol. 1, Ch. 1 – modern derivation and interpretation of the Dirac equation.
- H. Kragh, *Dirac: A Scientific Biography* – historical context of the 1928 papers and the positron prediction.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Assuming Dirac immediately identified holes with positrons; he first proposed protons as the holes, then revised his view after criticism.
- Treating hole theory as the modern explanation; QED has replaced the filled sea with field quantization.
- Thinking the Dirac equation is the final theory of the electron; it is a one-particle approximation that breaks down at high energies and requires QED.
- Confusing the Dirac gamma matrices with the Pauli matrices; the γ^μ are 4×4, not 2×2.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Stern–Gerlach 1922 protocol (the spin-½ phenomenon the Dirac equation explains).
- Pauli 1925 protocol (the exclusion principle behind the filled Dirac sea).
- Dirac 1927 protocol (the radiation theory that preceded QED).
- Born 1926 scattering protocol (non-relativistic scattering; compare with relativistic scattering later).

- [ ] I can derive the spin and g-factor of the electron from the Dirac equation.
- [ ] I can name what the modern QED treatment obscures about Dirac’s hole-theory reasoning.
