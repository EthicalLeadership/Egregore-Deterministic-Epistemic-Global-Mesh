# Protocol: Isaac Newton – *Principia*, Book III: *The System of the World* (1687)

**Chapter:** 13 – Gravitation  
**Textbook:** Halliday, Resnick, Walker – *Fundamentals of Physics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Newton’s laws (Protocol 2), Kepler’s laws, circular motion, and the inverse-square law from the textbook.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Cohen & Whitman translation (1999) – clear modern English with extensive notes. Not free.
- **Free alternative:** Motte translation (1729) – archaic language, some passages ambiguous. [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Principles_of_Natural_Philosophy_(1729))
- **Translation notes:** Same as Protocol 2. Use Cohen & Whitman for critical passages if possible.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Universal gravitational constant G | Newton deduces proportionality, not an equation with a constant. He expresses everything in ratios. |
| Measured mass of the Earth or Moon | His Moon test uses ratios of accelerations, not absolute forces |
| Field concept | Gravity is a force acting at a distance; there is no gravitational field mediating it |
| Modern value for Earth’s radius | His Moon test uses a slightly inaccurate Earth radius, but the ratio still holds convincingly |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Derive from Kepler’s three laws that a central force proportional to \(1/r^2\) implies elliptical orbits. Write this out.
- [ ] **Regulae Philosophandi:** Read the Rules of Reasoning in Philosophy at the start of Book III. These four rules justify his inference from terrestrial to celestial mechanics.
- [ ] **Phaenomena:** Read the six *Phaenomena* (essentially Kepler’s laws and astronomical observations).
- [ ] **Propositions:** Proceed to Propositions I–VIII. Proposition IV is the key: the Moon is kept in its orbit by the same force that makes objects fall on Earth.
- [ ] **Moon test:** Work through Newton’s Moon test calculation: convert his numbers (Earth’s radius, Moon’s distance, period) into SI units and check that the centripetal acceleration matches the \(1/r^2\) fall-off. Note that he uses ratios, not absolute values.
- [ ] **Universal law:** Read Proposition VII: the law of universal gravitation (every particle attracts every other with \(F \propto m_1m_2/r^2\)).
- [ ] **Critical note:** Newton deduces proportionality, not \(F = GMm/r^2\). The constant G is a later formalization.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Identify what Newton is arguing against — Cartesian vortex theory, which explained planetary motion via swirling fluid, not central force.
- [ ] **Reception task:** The *Principia* was recognized immediately as revolutionary, but universal gravitation was criticized as “occult” (action at a distance) by Continental mechanists. Acceptance was gradual.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

Newton’s universal gravitation: \(F \propto m_1m_2/r^2\). The modern equation \(F = GMm/r^2\) quantifies the proportionality; Cavendish later measured the density of the Earth, which allowed calculation of G, though he did not isolate G as a universal constant.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern equation treats gravity as a field with a universal constant. Newton’s reasoning is entirely about proportionalities and geometric limits. He never writes an equation with G; the modern form obscures the ratio-based, comparative nature of his argument.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Attributing “measuring G” to Cavendish; he measured the density of the Earth.
- Assuming Newton’s G is the same as the modern value; he didn’t know the mass of the Earth, only ratios.
- Overlooking that his Moon test depends on a slightly inaccurate Earth radius, but the result was still convincing at the time.
- Reading the geometric proofs as pure math; the physical insight is in the limit arguments.
- Skipping the *Regulae Philosophandi*; they are the epistemological foundation of his inference from Earth to heavens.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Cavendish experiment (for Earth’s density, which enables G).
- Einstein’s general relativity (deviation from Newtonian gravity).

## 8. Epistemic Safety Checklist (Post-Reading)

- [ ] I can state Newton’s gravitation law as he stated it: a proportionality, not an equation with G.
- [ ] I can identify at least one tool Newton lacked that I used to understand his result.
- [ ] I can describe the “occult” criticism and gradual acceptance of universal gravitation.
- [ ] I can name what the modern equation \(F = GMm/r^2\) obscures about Newton’s ratio-based reasoning.
- [ ] I have not used the phrase “basically the same as” or “just a primitive version of” in my notes.
