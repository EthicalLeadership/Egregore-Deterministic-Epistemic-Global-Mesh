# Primary-Source Pairings: Griffiths – *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*

**Purpose:** After mastering a chapter in the modern textbook, read the paired original source for historical depth. Use the warnings column to separate what still holds from what has been superseded. For step-by-step reading guidance, see the matching protocol in `.library/protocols/`.

| Textbook Chapter (Griffiths) | Topic | Primary Source | Specific Section | Enrichment Value | ⚠️ What to Watch For (Outdated Concepts, Notation Shifts) | Free Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Wave Function** | Probabilistic interpretation of ψ | Max Born, “Zur Quantenmechanik der Stoßvorgänge” (1926) | *Zeitschrift für Physik* 37, 863–867; the footnote in which ψ*ψ is read as a probability density | The moment quantum mechanics ceases to be a deterministic theory of individual trajectories and becomes a theory of statistical predictions. | Born does not yet state a general postulate; the rule appears as a footnote in a paper on scattering, not as an axiom; no measurement theory or ensemble interpretation is offered; wavefunction collapse is not discussed. | [Springer (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01397477) |
| **2. Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation** | Matter waves and the wave equation | Louis de Broglie, *Recherches sur la théorie des quanta* (1924) | Doctoral thesis (Sorbonne), Chapters 1–2; phase waves and the electron wavelength λ = h/p | The original proposal that material particles have an associated wave, giving the historical motive for the Schrödinger equation. | De Broglie’s reasoning is relativistic and tied to an internal “periodic phenomenon”; he does not derive a wave equation; the pilot-wave interpretation in the thesis is later abandoned by mainstream QM. | [HAL / tel.archives-ouvertes (original French)](https://tel.archives-ouvertes.fr/tel-00006807/document) |
| **3. Formalism** | Matrix mechanics and observables | Werner Heisenberg, “Über quantentheoretische Umdeutung kinematischer und mechanischer Beziehungen” (1925) | *Zeitschrift für Physik* 33, 879–893; replacement of classical coordinates by transition amplitudes | The founding paper of matrix mechanics: observables are encoded by arrays of transition quantities, not by functions on phase space. | The word “matrix” is not used; the multiplication rule is discovered but not named; the paper predates the Hilbert-space and operator formalism; the uncertainty principle is absent. | [Westlake lab mirror (English translation, PDF)](https://wucj.lab.westlake.edu.cn/Others/Heisenberg_Quantum_Mechanics.pdf) |
| **4. Quantum Mechanics in Three Dimensions** | Heisenberg uncertainty principle | Werner Heisenberg, “Über den anschaulichen Inhalt der quantentheoretischen Kinematik und Mechanik” (1927) | *Zeitschrift für Physik* 43, 172–198; the γ-ray microscope and the Δx Δp ≥ ℏ/2 relation | The first operational analysis of why conjugate variables cannot be simultaneously known, grounding the abstract commutation relations in measurement. | The argument uses a semi-classical microscope thought-experiment, not a derivation from the formalism; the term *anschaulich* (visualizable/intuitive) is contested; energy–time uncertainty is treated more loosely. | [Springer (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01397280) |
| **5. Identical Particles** | Pauli exclusion principle | Wolfgang Pauli, “Über den Zusammenhang des Abschlusses der Elektronengruppen im Atom mit der Komplexstruktur der Spektren” (1925) | *Zeitschrift für Physik* 31, 765–783; the fourth quantum number and the exclusion rule | The origin of the spin-statistics connection and the explanation of the periodic table’s shell structure. | Pauli introduces a new quantum number but does not identify it as electron spin (Goudsmit and Uhlenbeck, 1925–26); the antisymmetry principle is implicit, not yet a general wavefunction postulate. | [Springer (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02980631) |
| **6. Time-Independent Perturbation Theory** | Perturbation theory for stationary states | Erwin Schrödinger, “Quantisation as a Problem of Proper Values (Part III)” (1926) | *Annalen der Physik* 386, 109–139; perturbation of proper values and proper functions | Schrödinger extends wave mechanics to problems that are not exactly soluble — the ancestor of modern Rayleigh–Schrödinger perturbation theory. | No Dirac notation or abstract Hilbert-space language; the treatment is wave-mechanical and coordinate-based; degenerate perturbation theory is only sketched. | [Collected Papers on Wave Mechanics (PDF)](https://mwolf.pracownicy.uksw.edu.pl/MK/Schrodinger_Collected_Papers_on_Wave_Mechanics.pdf) |
| **9. Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory** | Quantum theory of radiation | Paul A. M. Dirac, “The Quantum Theory of the Emission and Absorption of Radiation” (1927) | *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 114, 243–265; quantization of the electromagnetic field and Einstein A/B coefficients | The birth of quantum electrodynamics: photons are created and annihilated by operators acting on a radiation field. | The theory is non-relativistic for matter and treats the electromagnetic field as a collection of oscillators; renormalization and the full QED perturbation expansion come later. | [Royal Society (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1927.0039) |
| **11. Scattering** | Born approximation | Max Born, “Quantenmechanik der Stoßvorgänge” (1926) | *Zeitschrift für Physik* 38, 803–827; scattering as a perturbation of free-particle wavefunctions | The first systematic treatment of quantum scattering: the Born approximation and the probabilistic interpretation of the scattered wave. | The paper predates the modern S-matrix and partial-wave formalisms; spin is ignored; the distinction between elastic and inelastic scattering is not yet general. | [Springer (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01397184) |
| **3. Formalism** | Full matrix mechanics and canonical commutation | Max Born & Pascual Jordan, "Zur Quantenmechanik" (1925) | *Zeitschrift für Physik* 34, 858–888; the matrix formulation, equations of motion, and [p,q] = iℏI | The completion of Heisenberg's spectral program: matrices are named, commutation relations are derived, and operator mechanics takes shape. | No Hilbert-space or Dirac notation; no Schrödinger wavefunctions; no uncertainty principle; the eigenvalue problem is framed in matrix algebra, not abstract operator theory. | [Springer (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01328531) |
| **4. Quantum Mechanics in Three Dimensions** | Space quantization and the Stern–Gerlach experiment | Walther Gerlach & Otto Stern, "Der experimentelle Nachweis der Richtungsquantelung im Magnetfeld" (1922) | *Zeitschrift für Physik* 9, 349–352; splitting of a silver atomic beam in an inhomogeneous magnetic field | The experiment that forced physicists to take discrete spatial orientations of angular momentum seriously; later reinterpreted as direct evidence of electron spin. | The original interpretation invoked orbital angular momentum/space quantization, not electron spin; no quantum theory of spin-½; the modern "spin-up/spin-down" reading is a retrojection; no Schrödinger equation. | [Springer (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01326984) |
| **2. Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation** | Electron diffraction and the wave nature of matter | Clinton Davisson & Lester Germer, "Diffraction of Electrons by a Crystal of Nickel" (1927) | *Physical Review* 30, 705–740; diffraction peaks and identification of λ = h/p for electrons | The decisive experimental confirmation of de Broglie's matter-wave hypothesis; shows that the Schrödinger equation describes real electrons. | The analysis uses classical crystal diffraction (Bragg's law) rather than a full quantum scattering calculation; no Born approximation or S-matrix; spin is ignored; predates modern electron-diffraction formalism. | [APS (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.30.705) |
| **Special Topic: Foundations** | Bell's theorem and the EPR paradox | John S. Bell, "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox" (1964) | *Physics Physique Физика* 1, 195–200; derivation of the original Bell inequality and the conflict with local hidden variables | The paper that turned the EPR philosophical debate into a quantitative, testable prediction; the foundation of quantum information and loophole-free Bell tests. | Bell's original inequality is not the symmetric CHSH form used today; assumes idealized perfect correlations; experimental loopholes (locality, detection, freedom-of-choice) are not addressed; "hidden variables" is used in a broad sense. | [APS (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysicsPhysiqueFizika.1.195) |
| **Special Topic: Relativistic Quantum Mechanics** | Relativistic electron equation and spin | Paul A. M. Dirac, "The Quantum Theory of the Electron" (1928) | *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 117, 610–624; derivation of the Dirac equation, relativistic invariance, and automatic emergence of spin-½ | The unification of special relativity and quantum mechanics for the electron; spin and the g-factor emerge automatically rather than being added by hand. | The positron is predicted but not yet identified as the electron's antiparticle (hole theory came later); no QED or renormalization; the theory is single-particle in a fixed field, not full interacting field theory; modern Fock-space interpretations came later. | [Royal Society (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1928.0023) |
