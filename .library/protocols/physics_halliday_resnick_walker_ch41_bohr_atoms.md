# Protocol: Niels Bohr – *On the Constitution of Atoms and Molecules* (1913)

**Chapter:** 41 – All About Atoms  
**Textbook:** Halliday, Resnick, Walker – *Fundamentals of Physics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Rutherford’s nuclear model, line spectra, Balmer formula, classical electron orbit.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Original published in *Philosophical Magazine*; English text.
- **Free access:** [Internet Archive (Philosophical Magazine)](https://archive.org/details/philosophicalma00goog)
- **Translation notes:** Also widely reproduced online.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Schrödinger equation | The electron is a classical particle in orbit; there is no wavefunction |
| Spin / Pauli exclusion principle | Multi-electron atoms cannot be explained; the model is strictly hydrogen |
| Quantum mechanics as a general framework | Bohr’s model is a deliberate hybrid — classical orbits plus quantum jumps |
| Concept of probability amplitude | Transitions are discrete jumps, not probability distributions |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Write the Balmer formula and sketch the hydrogen energy level diagram. Then set it aside.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read Part I: “Binding of Electrons by Positive Nuclei.”
- [ ] **Postulates:** Identify Bohr’s postulates:
  1. Electrons move in stationary orbits without radiating.
  2. The angular momentum is quantized: \(mvr = nh/2\pi\).
  3. Radiation occurs only when jumping between orbits: \(hf = E_i - E_f\).
- [ ] **Correspondence principle:** Identify the correspondence principle — Bohr’s epistemological anchor that quantum behavior must converge to classical physics at large quantum numbers. He used this to derive the quantization condition, not merely assert it.
- [ ] **Derive Rydberg:** Follow his derivation of the Rydberg constant from these postulates and check that it matches experiment.
- [ ] **Ad hoc nature:** Acknowledge the ad hoc nature of the postulates; Bohr was aware, and the paper ends with a call for a deeper theory.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Identify what Bohr is arguing against — the classical “death spiral” problem of Rutherford’s nuclear model, where electrons would radiate and crash into the nucleus.
- [ ] **Reception task:** Bohr’s model was immediately recognized as a breakthrough for hydrogen spectroscopy, but its ad hoc postulates were criticized. It was accepted as a phenomenological success, not a fundamental theory.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

Energy levels: \(E_n = -13.6 \text{ eV}/n^2\). The model correctly predicts the hydrogen spectrum but fails for multi-electron atoms.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern Schrödinger solution treats the electron as a probability cloud with no definite orbit. Bohr’s model is classical mechanics with quantum constraints. The modern form obscures the deliberate, principled hybridity of his approach — and the fact that he used the correspondence principle as a derivation tool, not an afterthought.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- Abraham Pais, *Niels Bohr’s Times* – context and development.
- David Griffiths, *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics* – for the correct Schrödinger solution of hydrogen.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Taking the model as literal truth; it’s a stepping stone, not the final quantum description.
- Omitting the correspondence principle; it is not optional — it is how Bohr justified the quantization condition.
- Trying to apply it to helium; it fails.
- Not appreciating how radical it was to abandon classical radiation in the stationary states.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- [ ] I can name what the modern Schrödinger solution obscures about Bohr’s classical-quantum hybrid reasoning.
