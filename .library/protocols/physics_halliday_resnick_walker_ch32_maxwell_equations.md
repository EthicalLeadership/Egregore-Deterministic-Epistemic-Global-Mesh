# Protocol: James Clerk Maxwell – *A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field* (1865)

**Chapter:** 30–32 – Electromagnetic Induction, Maxwell’s Equations, Magnetism of Matter  
**Textbook:** Halliday, Resnick, Walker – *Fundamentals of Physics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Faraday’s lines of force and induction (Protocols 8 and the Faraday induction pairing), Gauss’s law, Ampère’s law, vector calculus concepts.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society*, Vol. 155 (1865). Original in English; no translation needed.
- **Free access:** [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/philosophicaltra151roya)
- **Translation notes:** No translation needed, but the paper uses 20 scalar equations in component form rather than the modern four vector equations.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Vector calculus / vector notation | Maxwell uses 20 scalar equations in component form; the modern 4 vector equations are a later compression (Heaviside, Gibbs) |
| Relativity / Lorentz transformation | His theory is embedded in a mechanical luminiferous ether; the ether is a physical medium with vortices and idle wheels |
| Experimental confirmation of electromagnetic waves | Hertz’s experiments came in 1887; Maxwell’s theory was mathematically predictive but experimentally unconfirmed in 1865 |
| Displacement current as a physical discovery | It is a theoretical necessity to make Ampère’s law consistent with charge conservation; he did not observe it directly |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Write down Maxwell’s equations in modern vector form. Then set them aside — Maxwell never wrote them this way.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read the Introduction and Part I (The Electromagnetic Field — general relations). Notice his mechanical analogy: the field is a dynamical system with stresses and motions in the ether.
- [ ] **General equations:** Read Part III (General Equations of the Electromagnetic Field). Track the 20 scalar equations. Identify which ones correspond to the modern four vector equations.
- [ ] **Displacement current:** Focus on the displacement current: Maxwell introduces it to generalize Ampère’s law so that charge conservation holds. Note that this is a theoretical correction, not an experimental finding.
- [ ] **Electromagnetic theory of light:** Read Part VI (The Electromagnetic Theory of Light). Follow his derivation that light is an electromagnetic wave propagating at the speed \(c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}\) (in modern notation).
- [ ] **Compare:** Compare his equations to the modern form: identify what the vector calculus compression erases.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Identify what Maxwell is arguing against — action-at-a-distance theories and the separation of electricity, magnetism, and light into distinct phenomena.
- [ ] **Reception task:** The paper was largely ignored by the physics community until Hertz’s experimental confirmation (1887) and Heaviside’s vector reformulation (1880s). Maxwell died in 1879 without seeing wide acceptance.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

Maxwell’s equations (modern vector form):

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}, \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}, \quad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0, \quad \nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern vector calculus form presents the equations as abstract field relations. Maxwell derived them from a mechanical ether model with vortices, idle wheels, and stresses. The modern form erases the mechanical analogy that guided his discovery and makes the displacement current appear as a mathematical term rather than a physical correction to a dynamical medium.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- Bruce Hunt, *The Maxwellians* – on the reception and reformulation of Maxwell’s theory.
- James Clerk Maxwell, *A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism* – his own expanded exposition (1873).

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Expecting the modern 4 vector equations; Maxwell has 20 scalar equations in component form.
- Treating the displacement current as an experimental discovery; it is a theoretical consistency requirement.
- Ignoring the mechanical ether model; it is not a quaint error but the physical foundation of his derivation.
- Retrojecting relativity onto Maxwell; he believed in a physical ether with absolute rest.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Hertz’s experimental confirmation (1887).
- Lorentz’s electron theory and the removal of the ether (1890s).
- Einstein’s special relativity (Protocol 11) — the final removal of the ether.
