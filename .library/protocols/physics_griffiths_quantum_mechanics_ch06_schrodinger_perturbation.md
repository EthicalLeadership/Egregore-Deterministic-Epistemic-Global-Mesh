# Protocol: Erwin Schrödinger – *Quantisation as a Problem of Proper Values (Part III)* (1926)

**Chapter:** 6 – Time-Independent Perturbation Theory  
**Textbook:** Griffiths – *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*  
**Tier:** Essential  
**Prerequisites:** Time-independent Schrödinger equation, eigenvalue problems, first-order perturbation theory, the infinite square well and harmonic oscillator.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Schrödinger’s third paper in the series, *Annalen der Physik* 386 (18), 109–139 (1926). English translation in *Collected Papers on Wave Mechanics* (Blackie / AMS Chelsea).
- **Free access:** [Collected Papers on Wave Mechanics (PDF)](https://mwolf.pracownicy.uksw.edu.pl/MK/Schrodinger_Collected_Papers_on_Wave_Mechanics.pdf)
- **Translation notes:** “Eigenwert” and “Eigenfunktion” are translated as “proper value” and “proper function.” The modern terms are eigenvalue and eigenfunction.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Dirac / bra-ket notation | Perturbation theory is developed in coordinate representation with differential operators. |
| Abstract Hilbert-space projector formalism | Degenerate perturbation theory is treated case by case, not by diagonalizing a perturbation matrix in a degenerate subspace. |
| Feynman diagrams or Dyson series | The perturbation expansion is algebraic and wave-mechanical, not diagrammatic. |
| Renormalization | The paper deals with bound-state perturbations, not the divergences of quantum field theory. |
| Probability interpretation as a postulate | Schrödinger still regards ψ as a physical wave; the perturbation corrections are waves, not probability amplitudes in the modern sense. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Derive the first-order energy correction E_n^{(1)} = ⟨ψ_n^{(0)}|H'|ψ_n^{(0)}⟩ and the first-order state correction for a non-degenerate level.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read the introduction to Part III, where Schrödinger explains that the eigenvalue method now needs to be extended to problems that are not exactly soluble.
- [ ] **Close reading:** Follow the perturbation expansion of the wavefunction and the energy. Identify how the unperturbed eigenfunctions serve as a basis and how the small parameter is introduced.
- [ ] **Stark effect:** Read Schrödinger’s application to the Stark effect in hydrogen — the splitting of spectral lines in an electric field.
- [ ] **Derive:** Reconstruct the first-order correction to the energy of the hydrogen ground state in a uniform electric field and note that it vanishes by symmetry (linear Stark effect appears for excited states).
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Understand that Schrödinger is showing wave mechanics can do everything the old quantum theory did, and more: perturbation theory is a powerful computational tool.
- [ ] **Reception task:** Schrödinger’s perturbation theory was quickly adopted; the modern operator formulation is due to Dirac and later textbook writers.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

The modern result is time-independent perturbation theory: for H = H_0 + λH', the first-order energy shift is the expectation value of H' in the unperturbed state; higher orders follow from a recursive expansion.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern derivation uses abstract operator algebra and projectors. Schrödinger’s is a concrete differential-equation method; the perturbation is a small addition to a potential, and the corrections are found by expanding in the eigenfunctions of the unperturbed Hamiltonian.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- E. Schrödinger, *Collected Papers on Wave Mechanics* – contains the English translation of all four parts.
- J. J. Sakurai & J. Napolitano, *Modern Quantum Mechanics* – modern operator treatment for comparison.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Confusing Schrödinger’s “proper value” with the modern term “eigenvalue”; they are the same concept.
- Expecting a general treatment of degenerate perturbation theory as in Griffiths; Schrödinger handles special cases.
- Reading Born’s probability interpretation back into the paper; Schrödinger treats ψ as a real wave.

## 7. Follow-up / Cross-references

- Schrödinger 1926 Part I protocol (the unperturbed eigenvalue method).
- Born 1926 protocol (probability interpretation of the perturbed wavefunction).
- Dirac 1927 protocol (time-dependent perturbation theory and transitions).
