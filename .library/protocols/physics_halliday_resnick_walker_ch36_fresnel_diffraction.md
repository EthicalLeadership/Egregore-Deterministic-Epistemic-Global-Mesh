# Protocol: Augustin Fresnel – *Mémoire sur la diffraction de la lumière* (1818)

**Chapter:** 36 – Diffraction  
**Textbook:** Halliday, Resnick, Walker – *Fundamentals of Physics*  
**Tier:** Recommended  
**Prerequisites:** Wave interference, Huygens’ principle, single-slit and circular-aperture diffraction, geometric optics.

## 1. Edition & Translation

- **Recommended:** Fresnel’s 1818 prize memoir, submitted to the French Academy of Sciences on 29 July 1818 and crowned on 15 March 1819. An English translation appears in H. Crew (ed.), *The Wave Theory of Light: Memoirs by Huygens, Young and Fresnel* (American Book Company, 1900).
- **Free access:** [Académie des Sciences – original French PDF](https://www.academie-sciences.fr/pdf/dossiers/Fresnel/Fresnel_pdf/Mem1818_p339.pdf)
- **Translation notes:** The original uses early 19th-century French mathematical vocabulary; "onde" = wave, "rayon" = ray, "diffraction" already carries its modern sense. Crew’s English translation modernizes some notation but preserves the argument.

## 2. What the Author Did NOT Have

| Author Did NOT Have | Consequence for Reading |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic theory of light | Fresnel treats light as a mechanical wave in an elastic ether, not as coupled E and B fields. |
| Vector calculus or Maxwell’s equations | The theory is built from Huygens’ geometrical construction plus the principle of interference; integrals are scalar path-length integrals. |
| Transverse-wave theory (at first) | In 1818 Fresnel still assumed longitudinal waves for many diffraction calculations; transverse waves come in his later polarization work. |
| Precise wavelength standards | Wavelengths are inferred from diffraction measurements, not defined independently; expect proportionality arguments rather than absolute numbers. |
| Photon or quantum concepts | The wave theory is offered as the *only* viable theory, with no reconciliation to particle-like behaviour. |

## 3. Reading Steps

- [ ] **Pre-reading:** Sketch Huygens’ construction for a plane wave passing a straight edge. Predict where bright and dark fringes should appear according to geometric optics alone.
- [ ] **Primary source:** Read the introduction and Section I of the memoir, where Fresnel states his two fundamental hypotheses: (i) each point on a wavefront is the centre of secondary wavelets, and (ii) these wavelets interfere according to the principle of superposition.
- [ ] **Close reading:** Follow Fresnel’s derivation of the diffraction pattern produced by a narrow obstacle or aperture. Pay attention to how he sums the contributions of wavelets by considering path differences.
- [ ] **Poisson spot:** Locate the passage (or read Arago’s report) on the prediction that a bright spot should appear at the centre of the shadow of a small circular disc. Note that Poisson intended this as a *reductio ad absurdum* of wave theory.
- [ ] **Derive:** Re-derive the condition for the Poisson/Arago bright spot using modern Fresnel-zone language, then compare with Fresnel’s own geometrical reasoning.
- [ ] **Historical-context task:** Identify the intellectual opponents — Laplace, Biot, and Poisson were partisans of Newton’s corpuscular theory; Arago and Ampère supported the wave programme.
- [ ] **Reception task:** The 1819 Academy prize was awarded to Fresnel despite the corpuscular majority on the jury. By the late 1820s the wave theory had become dominant in France; Britain followed more slowly after Young’s work was re-evaluated.

## 4. Modern Analog / Retrojection

Fresnel diffraction is now described by the Fresnel–Kirchhoff integral; the Poisson spot is the on-axis constructive interference of wavelets bending around a circular obstacle.

**What is lost in this retrojection?** The modern integral treats the field as a scalar complex amplitude and assumes an electromagnetic wave. Fresnel’s reasoning is geometrical and mechanical, tied to an ether of finite elasticity; the physical picture of "secondary wavelets" is an intuition pump, not a field-theoretic calculation.

## 5. Secondary Sources

- E. Hecht, *Optics* – modern treatment of Fresnel and Fraunhofer diffraction.
- J. Z. Buchwald, *The Rise of the Wave Theory of Light* – historical context of the 1818 prize and the corpuscular-wave debate.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- Calling the bright spot "Poisson’s spot" as if Poisson discovered it; it was Fresnel’s prediction, Arago’s experimental verification, and Poisson’s attempted refutation.
- Assuming Fresnel’s 1818 memoir already uses transverse waves; transverse polarization enters his later work.
- Reading the modern Fresnel integral back into the memoir as if Fresnel wrote it in complex-exponential form.

## 8. Epistemic Safety Checklist (Post-Reading)

- [ ] I can state, in Fresnel’s own terms, how Huygens’ principle and the principle of interference are combined.
- [ ] I can identify at least one tool Fresnel lacked that I used to derive the diffraction pattern.
- [ ] I can describe the corpuscular opposition and how Arago’s experiment changed the jury’s verdict.
- [ ] I can name what the Kirchhoff–Fresnel integral obscures about Fresnel’s original reasoning.
- [ ] I have not used the phrase “basically the same as” or “just a primitive version of” in my notes.
